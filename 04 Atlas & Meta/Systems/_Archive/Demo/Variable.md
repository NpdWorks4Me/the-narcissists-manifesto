## Definition

> The *quality of variability* — unpredictability — is what makes a reward schedule habit-forming. Same total payoff, distributed variably, beats a fixed schedule on retention and craving. Nir Eyal's Hook model places the **variable reward** as the third phase (trigger → action → variable reward → investment); Skinner boxes proved the principle decades earlier. Operationally distinct from magnitude or frequency.

## Nuance

> Not all variability is equal. Tribe / hunt / self (Eyal's three reward types) have different ethical loads. Exploitative variability (slot-machine, infinite scroll) trains craving; empowering variability (discovery, mastery, surprise) trains engagement. Fixed schedules bore; *random* schedules frustrate — the sweet spot is structured unpredictability. See [[Behavioral Economics]] for the broader framing.
