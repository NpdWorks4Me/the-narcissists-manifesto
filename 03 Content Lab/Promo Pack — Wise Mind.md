## Social post — LinkedIn / Facebook (longer)

> Think about the last big decision you made. Were you in your head, or in your feelings?
>
> Most people pick one lane and stay there. Overthinkers gather facts and never decide. Reactors feel everything, act first, and apologize later.
>
> DBT calls the middle ground Wise Mind. It's the overlap between logic and emotion, where you feel your true feelings and still act with wisdom in response to them.
>
> The practice is simpler than it sounds. Name which voice runs your life. Relax your body. Replay the last time your feelings won, this time with compassion. Then ask: what would a wise response look like?
>
> One thing at a time. One meal with the phone away. One honest page. That's how the muscle grows.
>
> You're not broken for feeling a lot. You're just missing the overlap. It's trainable.
>

## Social post — Thread/X (7-post thread version)

1. Your brain has two gears. Logic gear and feeling gear.
2. Most people think you have to pick one. Overthinkers stay in their head forever. Reactors act first and apologize later.
3. DBT says there's a third place. It's called Wise Mind. It's where the two circles overlap.
4. In Wise Mind, you feel your feelings fully AND you still choose well. You see the gray area, not just black and white.
5. How you practice it: name which voice is loudest. Relax your body. Replay the last moment your feelings took over, this time with compassion.
6. Ask the question: "What would a wise response look like here?" Write down the answer. That's your gut and your mind agreeing.
7. Start with one thing at a time. One meal. One walk. One decision. The overlap is trainable. [link]

## Content note / fairness disclaimer (for publishing)

This is a self-guided skills piece, not therapy and not a diagnosis. It describes a pattern, not a verdict on any one person. Readers and viewers may be carrying anxiety or trauma, so keep the tone warm and non-judgmental: the emotional mind is never framed as a flaw. Relaxation and meditation can be hard for some people; if closing eyes feels bad, the exercise works with eyes open, and the writing prompts can be done alone. No label on anyone, including the reader, is needed to use the skill.
