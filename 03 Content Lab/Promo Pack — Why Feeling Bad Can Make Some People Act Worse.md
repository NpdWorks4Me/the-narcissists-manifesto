> If you do something wrong and feel awful about it, you usually stop. Those feelings are the brakes.
>
> But for some people, the brakes are wired backwards. Guilt, shame, and remorse don't slow them down. They make them lash out more.
>
> Three ways this happens: they blame you for their own feelings, they turn the guilt into "that's just who I am," or they hand the bad feeling to you so you end up carrying it.
>
> If you're always the one apologizing? Step back and check who actually did what. Love and letting someone hurt you aren't the same thing.

## Social post — Thread/X (7-post thread version)

1. We assume feeling guilty stops bad behavior. For most people, it does. It's the brakes.
2. But for some people, the brakes are wired backwards. Feeling bad makes them meaner.
3. Way it happens #1: they blame you. "You made me feel guilty." You didn't have to do anything.
4. Way #2: they turn guilt into identity. "That's just who I am." Real change means different action.
5. Way #3: they hand you the bad feeling. You walk away guilty even though they hurt you.
6. If you're always apologizing, answer this: who actually did what here?
7. Love and letting someone hurt you aren't the same thing. Protect your peace. [link]
