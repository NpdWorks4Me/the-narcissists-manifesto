# Reaction Formation and Projective Identification in Abuse

In the account presented in the source material, [[shame]], [[guilt]], and [[remorse]] are normally inhibitory emotions: they tend to stop abusive behavior rather than encourage it. However, when these emotions are experienced by people with high narcissistic defenses, psychopathic traits, dark personality styles, or conditions such as [[borderline personality disorder]], they can become triggers for externalized aggression and increased abuse. The source identifies three defense mechanisms that mediate this shift: alloplastic defenses, [[affective-matching|Affective Matching]], and [[reaction formation]]. ^[yt-xmowschonlc.md:9]

Alloplastic defenses operate by blaming the victim for the abuser’s own uncomfortable feelings. The abuser becomes angry at the person who “made” them feel guilty, ashamed, or remorseful, and then punishes that person. [[affective-matching|Affective Matching]] refers to the tendency to treat these negative emotions as the “cost of being me,” so the person continues the same behavior as an expression of authenticity, defiance, and self-acceptance. ^[yt-xmowschonlc.md:9]
