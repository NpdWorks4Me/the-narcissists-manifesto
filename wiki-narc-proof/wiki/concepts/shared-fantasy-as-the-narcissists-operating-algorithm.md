## Overview

The **shared fantasy** is described by Sam Vaknin as the narcissist’s operating algorithm. It dictates narcissistic behavior in an inexorable, unstoppable, uncontrollable, and immutable way, making the narcissist as much a puppet of the algorithm as the people in the narcissist’s life. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

The shared fantasy is the narcissist’s living space — a paracosm. Through it, the narcissist exercises external regulatory functions such as obtaining [[narcissistic supply]], and to some extent [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]]. By definition, a shared fantasy must be shared: without a participant, the narcissist cannot maintain or propagate it and collapses. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

## Abandonment anxiety and the shared fantasy

Narcissists are described as terrified of abandonment. This is a clinical feature shared with [[borderline personality disorder|borderline personality organization]], where the clinical term is separation insecurity. For the narcissist, however, abandonment anxiety is not focused on losing a particular individual. It is catastrophizing about the shared fantasy itself: being left alone and unable to provide self-supply or self-regulation in the long term, then falling apart. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

This form of abandonment anxiety borders on [[narcissistic mortification]] and is a major affective feature of [[narcissistic collapse]]. Abandonment acts as an indicator of an underlying foundational pathology. It reminds the narcissist how fragile and brittle his existence is, how delusional his universe is, and how divorced from reality he is. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

## Why the narcissist devalues and discards

The apparent contradiction — fearing abandonment but pushing people away — is resolved by the phases of the shared fantasy and by the distinction between idealized and devalued objects. The narcissist idealizes other people as maternal figures and converts them into “mothers.” Through [[idealization]] and love bombing, the narcissist becomes emotionally invested and attached, and dreads abandonment by the idealized object. At this stage, the narcissist resembles a borderline, whose attachment is to an idealized object rather than to the real person. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

When the shared fantasy moves into [[devaluation]] and [[discard]], the internal object is transformed from an idealized, unconditionally loving object into a persecutory object perceived as an internal enemy. The narcissist then dreads annihilation by this devalued persecutory object rather than abandonment, and actively craves abandonment by it. Abandonment becomes a welcome solution — a purging or cleansing of the internal space. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

## Twin anxieties and the difference from borderline

Twin anxieties are typical of [[cluster B]] personality disorders. Borderlines have abandonment anxiety plus [[engulfment-anxiety|Engulfment Anxiety]] when a partner becomes too intimate, leading to approach-avoidance repetition compulsion. Narcissists have abandonment anxiety in the idealization phase and annihilation anxiety in the devaluation phase. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

The psychodynamic difference is that, in borderline personality disorder, these twin anxieties drive behavior and are the cause of behavior. In narcissism, behavior is dictated by the shared-fantasy algorithm; the algorithm’s behaviors then bring on the twin anxieties. This is presented as a major psychodynamic difference between pure pathological narcissism and borderline personality organization. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

## Using abandonment as leverage

Vaknin’s main advice remains [[no contact]], cutting losses, and getting away. If that is impossible — emotionally, financially, or otherwise — he suggests using all available tools. During the idealization phase, abandonment can be used as an instrument: threatening abandonment, verbally or through body language and behavior, can modify narcissistic behavior, establish boundaries, strike compromises, and level the playing field. The mere mention of abandonment can transform the narcissist quickly. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]

This weapon is not available during the devaluation phase. A narcissist in devaluation wants the person gone; threatening abandonment would make the narcissist happy and euphoric, validate the view of the person as an enemy, and energize further defiance and aggression. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9-9]
