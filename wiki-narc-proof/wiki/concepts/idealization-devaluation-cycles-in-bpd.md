# Idealization-Devaluation Cycles in BPD

## Definition
In the source material, idealization-devaluation is described as a BPD relational pattern in which a person rapidly alternates between idealizing and devaluing an intimate partner. The transcript states that the person with BPD “tends to idealize and devalue” much like a narcissist, but that the cycling is “very rapid” in BPD: “she can idealize you and devalue you several times a day.” When idealizing, the partner is treated as “God”; when devaluing, as “the devil.” ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Characteristics
The source emphasizes that the cycle can repeat multiple times within a single day, unlike the slower idealization-devaluation pattern associated with narcissism. During the devaluation stage, the person “doesn’t feel bad” about betraying, cheating on, harming, or undermining the partner’s interests. The rapid cycling is presented as part of a wider picture of emotional dysregulation, mood instability, and impaired reality testing. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Relationship Context
The transcript connects these cycles to a broader dynamic in which the partner becomes the person’s “source of regulation.” The person with BPD is described as outsourcing internal “ego functions” and transferring her locus of control to the partner, making the partner responsible for her moods, emotions, cognitions, and behavior. The source calls this unhealthy and describes the resulting blame as scapegoating, noting that many of the person’s disruptive behaviors “have nothing to do with you as her partner.” ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Management Suggestions
The source advises partners to respond to these cycles by helping the person regain an internal locus of control, transferring responsibility back to her incrementally and without threats. It recommends rewarding “autoplastic defenses,” in which the person accepts responsibility for her actions, and rejecting “alloplastic defenses,” in which she blames others. The partner is advised not to accept responsibility for the person’s actions or moods, but to do so lovingly and kindly because the person is unwell. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]
