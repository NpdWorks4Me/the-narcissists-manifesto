## Definition

**Emotion dysregulation** is described as one of the key features of [[Borderline Personality Disorder]] (BPD), sometimes also called **affective liability**. It involves the inability to control emotions: emotions overwhelm and "drown" the person, leaving them unable to regulate themselves. The source places this feature second in a list of core BPD traits, after a pervasive sense of inner emptiness. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

## Relationship with anger

In BPD, emotion dysregulation often manifests as persistent anger. Unlike [[Narcissistic Rage]], which is reactive and only appears when a narcissist is challenged or undermined, the rage in BPD is described as "background noise" — it is always present. The person with BPD is depicted as constantly angry and on the verge of fury, even in otherwise calm settings. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

## In the broader BPD picture

The source notes that not every person with BPD exhibits all traits. Alongside emotion dysregulation, other key features include emptiness, suicidal ideation, self-harm, intense relationships with cycles of [[Idealization and Devaluation]], and the "twin anxieties" of [[Abandonment Anxiety]] and [[Engulfment Anxiety]]. Emotion dysregulation can be experienced by family members as unpredictable; for example, one account describes a mother's rage erupting suddenly during a quiet moment, followed by an expectation to "forget that ever happened." Such behavior may also connect to [[Splitting]] within family dynamics. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]
