# Amorality play

An **amorality play** is a concept used in the source material to describe the staged, theatrical quality of a psychopath’s relational abuse, particularly that of the female psychopath. In the psychopath’s amorality play, there is no good and evil, no right and wrong; there is only what works and what does not work. Where a typical [[morality play]] is Manichaean and structured as good versus evil, the amorality play is organized around efficacy and goal attainment. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

The female psychopath’s dramatic space is described as a theater set, a stage on which she enacts her antisocial amorality play. Everyone in her life becomes a prop in a production that is about her, her antisocial impulses, their gratification, and their elevation and mythologizing. She converts her psychopathy into an ideology, and within this dramatic space it becomes a private religion with everyone around her as a worshiper. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

The amorality play reduces people to actors in a theater production, or to two-dimensional NPCs in a video game. The only way the psychopath can accomplish this is by terrifying people, destabilizing them, and threatening them until they lose agency and come to rely on the psychopath. The [[narcissism|narcissist]] uses a similar technique to a much more minimal extent through [[intermittent reinforcement]]. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

The amorality play also serves the psychopath’s pursuit of power. Chaos renders the psychopath the only island of stability, making her indispensable and the only source of predictability and protection. The female psychopath is described as the sole producer and director of the script; the victim is an inert element in the drama, expected to be grateful for the role assigned to them, even as a sufferer. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]
