## Covert phase and the mask of sanity

The **covert phase** is a transitional state that occurs when a narcissist moves from a [[borderline self state]] into a [[psychopathic self state]] under conditions of extreme stress, anxiety, frustration, humiliation, or narcissistic injury. During this phase, the narcissist appears completely normal: he does not rage, is not overtly angry, and may seem reasonable, caring, or even loving. This apparent normalcy is sometimes described as the **mask of sanity**. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]

Despite the surface calm, the covert phase is marked by signs of underlying tension. The narcissist may be ponderous, brooding, spiteful, passive-aggressive, sarcastic, and bitter. He is determined yet evasive, denies there is any problem, and adopts an overly polite, pseudo-civil manner. He may be ostentatiously obedient or caring, but this behavior functions as an act rather than genuine engagement. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]

While wearing the mask, the narcissist continues to imagine the final act of the victim's destruction. He engages in detailed planning and obtains whatever tools are needed. This does not necessarily involve physical violence; it may include undermining or destroying a career, ruining the victim, or inflicting lasting trauma. The revenge fantasy is sometimes enacted covertly and sometimes overtly, and it is open to misinterpretation. The victim may believe they have the upper hand, but the damage is already taking effect internally, like a parasite. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]

The covert phase allows the narcissist to hide his intentions, premeditation, rage, hatred, and the conversion of the idealized object into an enemy. It serves as a bridge between two different kinds of narcissistic collapse: the eruptive borderline state — a sudden temper tantrum with no calm before the storm — and the escalatory borderline state, in which the narcissist provokes a fight through needling and projective identification, so that his own misconduct can be blamed on the victim's reaction. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]

Afterward, many narcissists transition into the psychopathic self state. At this point the conflict appears resolved: consensus has been restored, peace has been declared, and the victim feels safe. In reality, the narcissist is planning revenge and payback. He is described as a great actor who deceives the victim into complacency. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]

The narcissist's later aggression is justified through [[alloplastic defenses]], in which he blames others for his own behavior and adopts an [[external-locus-of-control|External Locus of Control]]. He claims, "You made me do it," and frames the conflict as a matter of survival: if he does not prevail, he will be destroyed. This survival mentality aggravates antisocial behavior. If he fails to win, he may sink into depressive or dysphoric moods, with substance abuse, withdrawal, and disruption of daily routines, eventually disintegrating into a prepsychotic stage. ^[yt-9me2rxhcyuq.md:9]
