# Semantic vs episodic psychological maturity

In discussions of [[cluster B personality disorders]], a distinction is drawn between **semantic** and **episodic** aspects of psychological functioning. The terms are sometimes confused, but they refer to different capacities. ^[yt-hko6ujraz7m.md:9]

**Semantic functions** concern the acquisition of skills and the ability to function in highly specific, rigid, structured, or specified environments. A person with high semantic capacity can learn languages, master new skills, acquire professions, rise to the top of a field, become a pillar of their community, and achieve major life accomplishments. This is described as “the semantic complex or the semantic executive functions.” ^[yt-hko6ujraz7m.md:9]

**Episodic aspects** involve life experience, biography, autobiography, a sense of continuity, and episodic memory. They also relate to how a person copes with the world and with other people, including the ability to perceive others as external objects — what the source calls [[object relations]]. ^[yt-hko6ujraz7m.md:9]

The distinction explains how someone can be highly accomplished in semantic terms yet remain psychologically immature. A person could be emotionally or psychologically “3 years old,” unable to cope with the world or perceive others as separate, while simultaneously being a leading physicist or the president of the United States. According to the source, the two capacities “have nothing to do with each other.” Psychological maturity is not a precondition for skilled functioning or [[executive functions|executive function]]. ^[yt-hko6ujraz7m.md:9]

This distinction is raised in a broader discussion of [[narcissistic personality disorder|narcissism]] and other cluster B conditions, where a person may be highly functional in semantic terms while lacking episodic/psychological maturity. ^[yt-hko6ujraz7m.md:9]
