## Freezing the Partner (Objectification Dynamic)

**Freezing the partner** refers to a dynamic in which one partner attempts to immobilize the other psychologically, reducing them to a stable, predictable, internal object rather than allowing them to be a living, changing, autonomous person. In relationships with a person with borderline personality disorder, the partner may come to be treated as a "fixture" or an "external regulator" whose sole purpose is to manage the borderline person's moods and emotions. This objectification renders the partner an instrument for the borderline person's emotional survival rather than a person in their own right. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

The borderline person's motivation for freezing the partner is rooted in a desperate attempt to ensure Object Constancy. Fearing abandonment, she needs the partner to remain completely static: not to evolve, not to grow, not to travel, not to have friends, and not to pay attention to anyone but her. The source describes this as wanting the partner to become "an ancient Egyptian mummy." This is comparable to, but distinct from, the narcissistic pattern: the narcissist freezes the partner because the partner might diverge from the narcissist's internal introject, while the borderline freezes the partner to prevent any change in the relational dynamic that could threaten the sense of security. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

This freezing strategy manifests as suffocating control: the borderline is "all over" the partner, monitors his attention, and reacts badly if he spends time with others, attends to work, or fails to respond instantly. The partner is effectively treated as property, expected to provide constant presence and attention. The borderline's hypervigilance means she senses the partner's withdrawal — the "silent creeping avoidance" — and recognizes that he is beginning to interact with an internal image of her rather than with her actual self. Even when she knows her behavior is harmful, she cannot stop, because she is dysregulated. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

For the partner, interacting with a real-life borderline partner requires what psychology calls "high-effort coping." As a self-preservation attempt, healthy partners may become "temporarily, transiently, situationally narcissists": they develop an internal snapshot of the borderline — reliable, caring, loving, non-dangerous — and interact with that snapshot instead of the external person. This internal object provides relief from the unpredictability and danger of the actual partner. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

The partner is thus pulled into both narcissistic and borderline dynamics. They use narcissistic defenses such as Introjection and introject constancy, while simultaneously developing borderline-like responses such as Engulfment Anxiety, avoidance, decompensation, and approach-avoidance behavior. They may develop anxiety about both the external object and the internal object — introject anxiety — and feel they do not know what to do. The partner wants agency and control over their life, and resists being deactivated or objectified, which fuels [[engulfment-anxiety|Engulfment Anxiety]]. Over time, even healthy partners can be "driven clinically to become personality disordered" by the dynamic. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

The borderline, perceiving the partner's withdrawal, feels abandoned and rejected. When borderlines feel this, their defenses crumble, they become secondary psychopaths, and they act out aggressively, recklessly, or immorally. This fuels the larger Approach-Avoidance Cycle: the borderline approaches, intimacy forms, then she fears abandonment and engulfment, withdraws, and later approaches again. The partner mirrors the cycle, creating a mutual, escalating system of objectification, avoidance, and anxiety. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]

In summary, "freezing the partner" is an objectification dynamic aimed at securing the borderline person's emotional stability by denying the partner's agency. It provokes the partner's own narcissistic and borderline defenses, undermines both partners' object constancy, and intensifies the abandonment and engulfment anxieties at the core of the relationship. ^[yt-vjdkwbh4fzy.md:9]
