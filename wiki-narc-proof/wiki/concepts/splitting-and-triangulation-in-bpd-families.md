## Splitting and Triangulation in BPD Families

**Splitting** and **triangulation** are relational patterns observed in families where a parent has [[Borderline Personality Disorder]]. In a first-person account of growing up with a borderline mother, splitting appears when one sibling is cast as “the good sibling” and another as “the bad sibling.” This polarized assignment is not fixed by actual behavior but by the parent’s emotional needs. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

**Triangulation** occurs when the parent constantly tries to form alliances with her own children, drawing them into conflicts and using them against one another. Rather than relating to each child directly and consistently, the parent pulls children into a shifting web of favor and disapproval. This can make family relationships feel unstable, as children are alternately recruited and rejected. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

Splitting and triangulation take place within the broader pattern of borderline traits described in the same account: chronic rage that operates as “background noise,” intense relationships marked by [[Idealization and Devaluation]], and cycles of approach and avoidance. The parent may idealize one child while devaluing another, then reverse those roles unpredictably. In the household described, this behavior was normalized as “just the way she is,” and family members were expected to accept it. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

The account also notes that not every person with borderline personality disorder exhibits all features. Splitting and triangulation are therefore illustrated as possible patterns in a borderline family, not universal markers of the disorder. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]
