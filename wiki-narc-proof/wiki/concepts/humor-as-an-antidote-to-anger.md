# Humor as an Antidote to Anger

**Humor as an Antidote to Anger** is a technique for defusing anger, described in the context of anger management for partners of individuals with [[Borderline Personality Disorder]]. The approach is presented alongside other strategies such as [[Cognitive Restructuring]] and strict communication protocols, and is introduced with the claim that "humor is the best antidote to anger." ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The core instruction is that when the angry person is upset, the partner should not mock her, invalidate her anger, minimize her anger, or minimize her as a person. Instead, the partner should say something humorous that "suddenly exposes the whole situation as irrational." When the person is amenable to humor, this is expected to diffuse the anger. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The technique is carefully distinguished from mockery or invalidation. The humor is directed at the irrationality of the situation rather than at the person or her feelings, and its effectiveness depends on the individual's receptivity: it works only "if she's amenable to humor." ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

In the source material, humor is part of a broader effort to help a person with borderline personality disorder manage anger and reduce the risk of acting out and [[Emotional Dysregulation]]. Other recommended techniques include cognitive restructuring, establishing rigid communication protocols with no personal attacks, and teaching the person to label and verbalize emotions. Humor functions as one accessible tool within that larger anger-management framework. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]
