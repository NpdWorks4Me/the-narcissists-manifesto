# [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] and Authoritarianism

**External regulation** is the use of other people to regulate and stabilize one's own emotions and moods. A person overwhelmed by emotions, or subject to labile moods, may turn to others to restore inner peace and harmony. This stands in contrast to [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]], the normal pattern in healthy people, who possess internal regulatory mechanisms, remain stable around an average, and treat emotions as signals rather than threats. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## Appeal of [[external-regulation|External Regulation]]

[[external-regulation|External Regulation]] creates dependency and an external locus of control: the regulated person becomes a "play thing," an object, and experiences the condition as ego-dystonic and somewhat menacing. Despite this, vulnerable people — including those with borderline personality disorder and narcissistic personality disorder — often choose external over [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]], even when [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]] is available, and show strong resistance to [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]] in therapy. The appeal lies in tradeoffs. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

First, [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] enables alloplastic defenses: failures, defeats, wrong decisions, and harms done to others can be blamed on an external regulator, shielding the person from shame and guilt.
