# CBT for Negative Automatic Thoughts in BPD

## Overview

[[Cognitive behavioral therapy]] (CBT) can be used to counter negative automatic thoughts in [[borderline personality disorder]] (BPD). The source describes these thoughts as leading to catastrophizing, despair, depression, and anxiety. In this view, the individual with BPD tends to assume the worst, which deepens emotional distress. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9-9]

## Negative automatic thoughts in BPD

Negative automatic thoughts are described as a central cognitive feature of BPD. They are said to lead directly to catastrophizing and to further depressive and anxious states. Anxiety and depression are described as frequently comorbid with BPD and often result from these unchecked negative thought patterns. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9-9]

## CBT approach

CBT is described as very successful at eliminating automatic negative thoughts. The recommended approach includes learning specific techniques for identifying and countering these thoughts, or attending therapy sessions focused on them. The source also connects CBT with broader skills such as labeling emotions, dialoguing with anxieties and fears, verbalizing internal experiences, and externalizing emotions behaviorally. Together, these methods are said to reduce the risk of acting out, [[emotional dysregulation]], and compensatory behavior. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9-9]

## Related techniques in BPD treatment

In the source, CBT is presented as part of a wider strategy for managing BPD symptoms. This includes [[cognitive restructuring]] for anger management, communication protocols to reduce blame and personal attacks, humor to defuse anger, and routines and stress management to address mood swings. It also includes helping the individual regain an internal [[locus of control]] rather than outsourcing emotional regulation to others. These techniques are complementary to, rather than a substitute for, CBT work on negative automatic thoughts. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9-9]
