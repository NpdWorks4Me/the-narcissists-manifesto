# Psychopathic vs narcissistic sexuality

The source material contrasts psychopathic and narcissistic sexuality within a broader discussion of [[female-psychopathy|Female psychopathy]], describing how each condition approaches sex, power, and seduction differently.

## Core distinction: coercion vs. irresistibility

The central difference drawn in the source is coerciveness. The psychopath's sex is usually aggressive, sometimes violent, and coercive in many cases, because the psychopath needs to be in control, needs to own, and needs to possess. The narcissist's sex, by contrast, is not coercive, because the narcissist needs to feel irresistible. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## Sex as an instrument of power

For psychopaths, sex is ultimately a form of power. The source states that "money is power. Sex is power. Sex is having power over someone," and notes that the female psychopath's goal is no different from the male psychopath's. Power functions as the psychopath's narcissistic supply — the fuel that keeps a psychopath going. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## Gender differences in expression

Male psychopaths tend to be factor-one (primary) psychopaths who are goal-oriented: "he wants sex, he wants money." Female psychopaths tend to be factor-two (secondary) psychopaths, marked by impulsivity, emotion dysregulation reminiscent of borderline personality disorder, recklessness, and defiance. Female psychopaths abuse people relationally, within interpersonal relationships — in this sense they are very reminiscent of the male narcissist. This includes smear campaigns and cheating with a partner's best friend, colleague, or their spouses. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## Seduction and role-playing

The female psychopath's sexuality is a lure. She presents herself as "the long-sought soulmate, the twin flame, perfect resonance and fit, the other long-lost half" — her sex is "fantasy come true, fantasy in the flesh."
