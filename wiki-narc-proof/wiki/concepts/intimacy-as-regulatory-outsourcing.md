# Intimacy as Regulatory Outsourcing

**Intimacy as regulatory outsourcing** is a concept describing intimate relationships as a form of external regulation — the use of other people to regulate and stabilize one’s own emotions and moods. The idea is presented in the context of a broader discussion of [[external-regulation|External Regulation]], where intimacy is generalized as a form of regulatory outsourcing rather than a purely emotional bond. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] and [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]]

[[external-regulation|External Regulation]] occurs when a person who is overwhelmed by emotions or suffers from labile moods turns to other people to restore equilibrium. By contrast, healthy, normal people are said to regulate from the inside: they have internal regulatory mechanisms, do not experience extreme highs or lows, and treat emotions as signals rather than threats. Emotion dysregulation and mood liability are highlighted as key clinical features of conditions such as borderline personality disorder. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## Why [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] is appealing

Despite its costs — including dependence on the external regulator, an external locus of control, and a sense of being controlled from outside — [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] offers apparent benefits. These include alloplastic defenses, which allow a person to blame others and avoid shame and guilt; an anxiolytic effect, because responsibility and accountability are handed over to someone else; and relief from remorse or regret. The source compares this dynamic to the pact between populations and dictatorial or authoritarian leadership: the leader takes responsibility, and in exchange the follower surrenders power. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## Regulation in groups, peers, and narratives

[[external-regulation|External Regulation]] is common in collective settings. In groups, the locus of control resides in the group itself, which regulates members’ behaviors, cognitions, emotions, and decisions, producing a “mob mind” or “cult mind.” peer pressure is described as a form of [[external-regulation|External Regulation]], in which peers communicate expectations and punish non-conformity. [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] is also mediated through narratives and symbols — for example, religion, morality plays, and conspiracy theories — where control is relegated to a symbolic realm. These narratives reduce anxiety, absolve the individual of responsibility, and enable integration into a group. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## Intimacy and regulatory outsourcing

Intimacy is said to involve two processes: the exposure and communication of vulnerability, and the handing over of power or control to another individual. From this perspective, intimacy is itself a form of regulatory outsourcing, and it appears in dyads and collectives alongside a modicum of [[external-regulation|External Regulation]]. The source notes an irony: people with narcissistic personality disorder are typically incapable of true intimacy and instead replace it with fantastic intimacy — an intimacy that exists in a shared fantasy or internal narrative. When the narcissist becomes embedded in such a fantasy, they may become regulated from the outside through sources of narcissistic supply and through the shared fantasy itself. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]

## Pathological forms and the line between intimacy and dependence

When [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] becomes intense and all-pervasive, it can manifest as pathology. In borderline personality disorder, the intimate partner is expected to regulate emotions and moods, as well as needs and expectations. In codependence — referred to in the source as dependent personality disorder — the partner is expected to regulate the narcissist’s self-concept through self-enhancement or narcissistic supply. In healthy relationships, some degree of [[external-regulation|External Regulation]], exchange of agency, and dependency may be present; when it enhances well-being and allows separate lives to continue, it is called intimacy. In other situations, it becomes dependence, which can deteriorate into coercion, fantasy, or worse. ^[yt-enjcx2uswho.md:9]
