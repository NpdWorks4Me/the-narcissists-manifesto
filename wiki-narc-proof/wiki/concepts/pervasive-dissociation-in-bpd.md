# Pervasive Dissociation in BPD

Pervasive dissociation is described as one of the psychodynamic processes underlying avoidant behavior in borderline personality organization. In the source material, the borderline's avoidance is presented as driven by a confluence of pathological processes, and pervasive dissociation is named as the fourth such dynamic process. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9]

## Manifestations

The source identifies three core manifestations of pervasive dissociation: bouts of amnesia, derealization, and depersonalization. The person feels empty and unreal. This experience is especially likely when the person is alone; the source notes that borderlines have a very low threshold for tolerating loneliness. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9]

Dissociative experiences also occur during sex, particularly acting-out sex such as cheating or promiscuity. In such states, the person may feel on autopilot and denuded of any inner content. The source illustrates depersonalization with the internal statement: "I'm observing my body doing this, but it's not me. I'm not there. I'm not inside." Derealization is expressed as: "This is not real. It's like a dream. It's like a nightmare. It's going to be over soon." Amnesia appears as: "I'd better forget that this had happened." ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9]

## Relation to identity disturbance

Pervasive dissociation is closely linked to identity disturbance. Identity disturbance involves fluctuating between mutually exclusive beliefs, values, behavior patterns, cognitions, and emotions, owing to an absence of a core identity — described in the source as a void or black hole. The source states that pervasive dissociation "has a lot to do with" identity disturbance. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9]

## Context: acting out and avoidance

In the source, pervasive dissociation is situated within a broader account of the borderline's avoidance, which is often coupled with decompensation and acting out. Acting out may take reckless forms such as promiscuity, shopaholism, workaholism, or substance abuse, and is described as a way of protecting the self from overwhelming, labile emotions. During acting out, a "psychopathic self-state" is said to take over, and the partner is no longer in the person's mind, consistent with the phenomenon of "out of sight, out of mind" or object inconstancy. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9]
