# Narcissistic Decompensation

**Narcissistic decompensation** is the process by which a person relying on narcissistic defenses, when subjected to stress beyond their capacity to absorb, falls back into an earlier borderline state. Triggers include narcissistic mortification, narcissistic injury, and other intense tension, anxiety, or stress. In decompensation, the narcissist’s defenses crumble, they begin to feel and emote, and the first emotion is typically shame. At this point, the person becomes clinically indistinguishable from someone with borderline personality disorder, showing emotional dysregulation, suicidal ideation, and self-harming behavior. ^[yt-qatgve1-k6m.md:9]

The false self arrangement is described as the “narcissistic solution”: a solution of not being, in which existence is outsourced to a false self. The source describes a small child, around two years old, in a borderline phase of terror and pain, who decides to vanish in order to stop being tortured and to invent a protector god. In this account, all children first become borderlines and then attempt to become narcissists; those who succeed become narcissists, while those who fail remain borderlines. This explains why severe stress causes the narcissist to default back to a borderline state. ^[yt-qatgve1-k6m.md:9]

The source cites two theoretical supports for this view. Groschstein said that “borderlines are failed narcissists,” and Kernberg described narcissistic personality disorder as a compounded defense against underlying borderline dynamics. Both scholars are presented as believing that narcissism is effectively another phase of borderline personality disorder, and the speaker of the source explicitly concurs. ^[yt-qatgve1-k6m.md:9]

Narcissistic defense is also described as the mirror image of borderline functioning. Whereas the borderline is more psychotic, confuses internal and external, and projects internal objects onto external ones, the narcissistic defense appropriates external objects by internalizing them, thereby neutralizing their threat. The borderline neutralizes threat by idealizing the external object; the narcissist neutralizes it by “swallowing,” digesting, or incorporating the object. The genesis of the false self is likewise opposite: the borderline false self is initially external and later internalized, while the narcissistic false self is initially internal and later acquires external capacity, such as interaction with society. ^[yt-qatgve1-k6m.md:9]
