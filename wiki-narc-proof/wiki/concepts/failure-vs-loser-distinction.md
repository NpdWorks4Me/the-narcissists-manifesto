# Failure vs. Loser Distinction

**Failure vs. Loser Distinction** is a conceptual pair articulated by Sam Vaknin. In this framing, *failure* and *loser* are not synonyms: a failure is someone who does not attain success, while a loser is someone who achieves successes but cannot convert them into lasting outcomes. Vaknin introduces the distinction at the start of a discussion on narcissistic abuse and asks the listener to bear it in mind as the discussion proceeds. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

## Failure

A failure is someone who never attains success. Such a person tries hard repeatedly but invariably misses the mark, for a variety of reasons, some of which are beyond the failure's control. In Vaknin's terms, a failure "just doesn't make the cut." ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

## Loser

A loser is someone who fails to translate serial successes into long-lasting, long-term beneficial outcomes. Unlike the failure, the loser does experience success from time to time, but fails to leverage it. The loser does not use accomplishments to establish permanent fixtures in real life, such as maintaining a career, sustaining a successful marriage, or raising children appropriately. In short, the loser is not efficacious. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

## Role in the discussion of narcissistic abuse

Vaknin presents this distinction as a preliminary conceptual tool in a larger analysis of [[narcissistic supply]] and [[narcissistic abuse]]. He describes the narcissist as contemptuous of others yet dependent on them for attention and adulation, creating resentment and rage. The failure-versus-loser distinction is offered as a lens for understanding these dynamics, and the source does not explicitly classify the narcissist as either a failure or a loser. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

Related concepts include [[narcissistic supply]], [[narcissistic abuse]], [[shared fantasy]], [[object constancy]], [[idealization and devaluation]], and [[borderline personality organization]].
