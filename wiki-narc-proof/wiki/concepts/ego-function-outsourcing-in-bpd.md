# Ego Function Outsourcing in BPD

## Definition

In Borderline Personality Disorder, "ego function outsourcing" refers to the patient’s reliance on an intimate partner to perform the internal regulatory functions she cannot provide for herself. The partner becomes "her source of regulation," described as "the one with the hand on the key," controlling her feelings, emotions, moods, reactions, explosions, and love. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The person with BPD is said to lack a regulated inner world, described in the source as "one gigantic twister." She effectively tells the partner: "Help me by regulating me. Be my external control. Be my external board of control. Be my user manual. I'm transferring my locus of control to you." ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

This outsourcing may be expressed as: "And from now on, you are my God. You are in charge of my moods, my emotions, my cognitions, my happiness, my unhappiness, my aggression. Everything I do, you are to blame. Everything I do, you're responsible and accountable for. Even if I misbehave, I misbehave because of you." ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Why It Is Unhealthy

The outsourcing is described as "extremely unhealthy," first because it is counterfactual: many of the patient’s disregulatory behaviors, emotions, and moods have nothing to do with the partner. Attributing everything to the partner effectively amounts to scapegoating the partner. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Restoring an Internal Locus of Control

The recommended response is to teach the patient to regain the locus of control and to transfer the responsibilities she had abrogated back to her. This should be done incrementally and gradually, without threats, demands, control, chastising, or criticism. The partner should be kind because "she is not well," and should help her recover by providing increasing control over herself, her circumstances, choices, decisions, and gradually her emotions and moods. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The partner should also help her develop and reward autoplastic defenses, which involve accepting responsibility for one’s actions and their consequences. By contrast, alloplastic defenses involve blaming other people for everything, implying an [[external-locus-of-control|External Locus of Control]]. To regain control of her life, the patient must accept responsibility for what she has done and for what happens to her. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

A simple language intervention is suggested: when the patient says "this happened to me," the partner can respond that it did not happen to her — "you did it." The source gives the example of the patient saying, "I was drunk and I slept with another man. It happened." The partner should respond: "So it did not happen. You did it." This is presented as restoring a sense of responsibility, self-control, and internal locus of control. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The partner is advised to reward responsible behavior when warranted, and when the patient does something bad, to refuse to accept responsibility and reject any attempt to place blame on the partner. The partner is not responsible for her actions, moods, emotions, cognitions, misbehavior, aggression, or violence. The refusal should be done lovingly, "because you love her." ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Related Features

Like the narcissist, the person with BPD tends to idealize and devalue, but the cycles are much more rapid — she can idealize and devalue the partner several times a day. When idealizing, the partner is "God"; when devaluing, the partner is "the devil." During the devaluation phase, she may not feel bad about betraying, cheating on, harming, or undermining the partner’s interests. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Clinical Context

The source frames ego function outsourcing within a broader discussion of emotional dysregulation in BPD. The patient’s inner world lacks regulation, and her moods are mostly reactive rather than internally generated. Her reality testing is impaired, so her perceptions are often wrong, deformed, or inaccurate. Restoring reality testing is said to reduce mood liability considerably, and reducing environmental triggers and stress can interrupt a chain reaction in which stress leads to anxiety, and anxiety leads to mood lability. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

Other recommended interventions discussed alongside the outsourcing concept include externalizing emotions behaviorally and verbally, chair work, cognitive behavioral therapy to counter negative automatic thoughts and catastrophizing, cognitive restructuring as an anger management technique, strict communication protocols, and humor to diffuse anger. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]
