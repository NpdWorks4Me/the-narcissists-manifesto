# Weaponizing Abandonment Anxiety as Relationship Leverage

Psychologist and author Sam Vaknin describes a technique for using a narcissist’s abandonment anxiety as leverage inside a toxic relationship, with the goal of mitigating the most egregious forms of [[narcissistic abuse]]. He frames the strategy as a kind of blackmail: the partner signals, verbally or behaviorally, that the narcissist may be abandoned if they continue to transgress boundaries, refuse compromise, or become overbearing, aggressive, or violent. Vaknin nevertheless repeats that his main advice remains no contact, and presents this leverage only for people who cannot leave for objective or subjective reasons. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Psychodynamics

Abandonment anxiety is a clinical feature shared by pathological narcissism and [[borderline personality disorder|borderline personality organization]]. In borderline individuals, separation insecurity is proactive and dynamic: the borderline imagines and anticipates abandonment, then reacts as though it had already occurred. In narcissism, the fear is a general, ambient dread of being left alone, because without a [[shared fantasy]] the narcissist cannot maintain [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] through narcissistic supply or [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]]. The shared fantasy requires at least one participant, so abandonment anxiety in the narcissist is catastrophizing about the collapse of that shared fantasy rather than about losing any particular person. Vaknin links this to narcissistic mortification and to [[narcissistic collapse]]. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

Vaknin identifies twin anxieties in narcissism: abandonment anxiety during the idealization phase and annihilation anxiety during the devaluation phase. In borderline personality disorder, these anxieties drive behavior. In narcissism, behavior is instead dictated by an algorithm called the shared fantasy, and the twin anxieties are brought on by that behavior. This distinction is central to understanding why the leverage works only at certain phases. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Why the narcissist pushes people away despite fearing abandonment

The apparent contradiction is resolved by recognizing that the narcissist only dreads abandonment by an idealized maternal object. During [[idealization and devaluation|idealization]], the narcissist converts the intimate partner into a mother figure, becomes emotionally invested, and is terrified of losing that idealized object. When devaluation begins, the internal object is transformed into a persecutory enemy. At that point, the narcissist craves abandonment by the devalued object and perceives it as a purging and cleansing of internal space. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Using abandonment anxiety as leverage

During the idealization phase, the threat of abandonment can be used as an instrument to modify the narcissist’s behavior, strike compromises, establish and enforce [[boundaries]], and reach a modus vivendi. Vaknin says the mere mention of abandonment can produce an immediate transformation, because the narcissist is attached to the idealized maternal object and fears losing it. He describes this technique as a kind of blackmail carried out under the cloud and threat of imminent abandonment. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Limitations and contraindications

This leverage is not available in the devaluation phase. Once the narcissist has devalued the partner, they want the partner gone. Threatening abandonment at that point makes the narcissist happy, euphoric, and elated, and it validates their view of the partner as the enemy. It energizes the narcissist and can make them more defiant, reckless, aggressive, demanding, and threatening. The weapon therefore works only while the narcissist still idealizes the target. ^[yt-82nfs
