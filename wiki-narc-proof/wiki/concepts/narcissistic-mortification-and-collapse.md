# Narcissistic Mortification and Collapse

**Narcissistic mortification** and **narcissistic collapse** are psychopathological states described in the context of pathological narcissism, particularly in relation to the narcissist’s dread of abandonment and the disruption of the **shared fantasy** that sustains their inner world.

## Narcissistic Mortification

Narcissistic mortification is closely linked to the fear of abandonment. When a narcissist faces the prospect or actuality of being left alone, the experience "has echoes of what we call narcissistic mortification." It lies "on the border between narcissistic injury and narcissistic mortification" and constitutes "a major affective feature of the process or the state of narcissistic collapse." ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

The narcissist dreads this state because abandonment and its aftermath remind them "how fragile and brittle his existence is, how fantastic and delusional his universe, his world is, and how divorced he is from reality." This painful self-awareness can precipitate a collapse. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Narcissistic Collapse

Narcissistic collapse occurs when the narcissist is unable to maintain and propagate the shared fantasy—the "living space" of the narcissist and the mechanism through which they regulate themselves externally (via narcissistic supply) and internally. In the absence of a participant for the shared fantasy, the narcissist collapses. The state of post-abandonment is compared to a fever: it is "an indicator of an underlying malaise, of a foundational pathology." ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

The abandonment anxiety in narcissism is a form of catastrophizing, but not about a specific person. Rather, it is "catastrophizing about the shared fantasy": the narcissist fears being left alone and "unable to provide myself with self-supply in the long term, unable to regulate myself." This catastrophe leads to the breakdown of the narcissistic world and the onset of collapse. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Abandonment Anxiety and the Shared Fantasy

The narcissist’s fear of abandonment stems from a childhood relationship with a maternal figure who did not allow separation and individuation. As a result, the narcissist develops a terror of abandonment, shared with borderline personality organization. However, in narcissism, the fear is ambient: it is a dread of being alone because solitude prevents the maintenance of a shared fantasy, which requires at least one other participant. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

During the **idealization** phase, the narcissist idealizes the intimate partner as a maternal figure and becomes emotionally attached—a state that resembles borderline dynamics. At this stage, the narcissist is terrified of abandonment by the idealized object. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Twin Anxieties in Idealization and Devaluation

As the narcissist transitions to **devaluation** and **discard**, the internal object undergoes a transformation: the idealized, unconditionally loving object becomes a "persecutory object" immersed in enmity. The narcissist then perceives the devalued person as an internal enemy and actually "craves abandonment and rejection by the devalued object," viewing abandonment as "a purging, a cleansing, an act of cleansing." ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

Thus, the narcissist possesses **twin anxieties**: abandonment anxiety during the idealization phase, and annihilation anxiety (the dread of being destroyed by the internal persecutory object) during the devaluation phase. These anxieties are not the cause of narcissistic behavior; rather, behavior is dictated by the "algorithm" of the shared fantasy, which is "inexorable, unstoppable, uncontrollable and immutable." The twin anxieties emerge as consequences of that algorithm-driven behavior. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

This contrasts with borderline personality disorder, where twin anxieties (abandonment and engulfment) actively drive behavior. In the narcissist, the anxieties are secondary to the shared-fantasy process. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Leveraging Abandonment Anxiety in Relationships

In the idealization phase, the narcissist’s fear of abandonment can be used as a tool to influence behavior and establish boundaries. Threatening abandonment—verbally, through body language, or behaviorally—can produce an immediate and dramatic change in the narcissist’s conduct. The mere mention of abandonment is sufficient to "completely transform the narcissist into someone unrecognizable." ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

This strategy fails during the devaluation phase. Threatening abandonment at that point "would make him very happy and euphoric and elated," because the narcissist wants the devalued object gone. It would validate the narcissist’s view of the person as an enemy and energize further defiance, aggression, and recklessness. The same threat therefore has opposite effects depending on the phase of the shared fantasy. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]
