## Overview

Innate emptiness is described as the first significant "tell" of borderline personality disorder (BPD). In the source, it is characterized as "a sense of innate emptiness ... a void, a black hole that is all consuming from the inside." This positions emptiness as a central internal experience rather than merely an observable behavior. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]

## Position among BPD traits

The source lists innate emptiness as the first of several key features of BPD. The other features mentioned are:

- Emotion dysregulation or affective liability
- Constant suicidal ideation and self-harm
- Chronic anger and rage
- Intense relationships involving idealization, devaluation, and approach-avoidance cycles
- Twin anxieties: abandonment anxiety and [[engulfment-anxiety|Engulfment Anxiety]]

The source also notes that not every person with BPD exhibits all of these traits. Innate emptiness is therefore presented as one core feature within a broader symptom constellation, not as a standalone criterion. ^[yt-9h0eep4spmm.md:9]
