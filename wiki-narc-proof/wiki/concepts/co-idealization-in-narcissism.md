## Definition

**Co-idealization** is a process in pathological narcissism in which the narcissist idealizes another person — usually an intimate partner or a source of [[narcissistic supply]] — as an indirect way of idealizing themselves. When the narcissist’s partner is perceived as extraordinary, attractive, intelligent, or unique, the narcissist feels equally extraordinary because the partner is regarded as their possession or property. The source compares this to owning a luxury car: possessing the object elevates the owner’s status and self-image. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

## Mechanism

Co-idealization is not genuinely about the partner. By idealizing another person, the narcissist ameliorates his or her own narcissistic wound and enhances [[grandiosity]]. Idealization thus functions as a form of self-idealization: the narcissist elevates others in order to feel good about himself or herself. This dynamic is described as an integral part of the idealization phase in narcissistic relationships. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

## Role of Narcissistic Abuse

Narcissistic abuse, counterintuitively, can also serve the co-idealization process. The abuse operates as a test of the partner’s idealized qualities: strength, cohesion, resilience, dedicated commitment, faithfulness, and capacity to love. If the partner survives the abuse and remains in the relationship, this is taken as proof that those idealized dimensions are real rather than imaginary. The partner is thereby rendered unique, inordinately resilient, and worthy of the narcissist’s equally godlike attributes. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]

In the broader framework, narcissistic abuse is closely connected to obtaining [[narcissistic supply]], to [[idealization and devaluation]] dynamics, and to the narcissist’s rage at being dependent on people he or she holds in contempt. Classifying the specific function of the abuse — whether it is testing unconditional love, co-idealizing the partner, devaluing, or expressing resentment over dependency — has practical therapeutic and behavioral implications, including helping targets design coping strategies. ^[yt-drlgalrra9k.md:9]
