# Female psychopathy

**Female psychopathy** refers to psychopathy as it manifests in women. According to the source, the majority of people diagnosed with psychopathy or antisocial personality disorder are men, with women making up only a small minority — between one-quarter and one-third of cases. This contrasts with narcissistic personality disorder, where women now constitute about half of all narcissists. Psychopathy is measured using the PCL-R, a test designed by Robert Hare. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## Primary vs. secondary psychopathy

Male psychopaths tend to be **factor one**, or **primary psychopaths**. Female psychopaths, by contrast, tend to be **factor two**, or **secondary psychopaths**. They are described as highly impulsive, lacking impulse control, emotionally dysregulated in ways reminiscent of borderline personality disorder, reckless, and defiant. Unlike primary psychopaths, female psychopaths are said to have emotions that are overwhelming, and may even experience empathy and negative affects such as shame and guilt. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## The "[[crazy-making-space|Crazy-making space]]"

The female psychopath is said to create a **crazy-making space**, described as the equivalent of the narcissist's shared fantasy. She lures, baits, and assimilates people into this space, which acts as a vortex or whirlpool that draws in ever-increasing concentric circles of people. She abuses people relationally — through smear campaigns, cheating with friends, colleagues, or their spouses, and by manipulating others through flirting or by begging for pity and sympathy, casting men in rescuer or savior roles. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

She may play many roles: the long-sought soulmate or twin flame, the shy and vulnerable damsel in distress, the codependent, or the extroverted, histrionic sexpot. These roles are described as cosplays that do not reflect her true character, and she can switch between them. Her chaos functions as an attractor; while each individual trait may seem unremarkable, their combination creates a compelling, intoxicating influence. Signs that one may be in the presence of a female psychopath include inexplicable influence, drama, acting out, unpredictability, and a subtle ambient menace. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## The goal: power

The goal of the female psychopath is the same as the male psychopath's: **power**. Money is power, and sex is power. Psychopathic sex is described as aggressive, sometimes violent, and often coercive, whereas narcissistic sex is not coercive because the narcissist needs to feel irresistible. Power is the narcissistic supply of the psychopath — the fuel that keeps her going. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

The function of the psychopath's [[crazy-making-space|Crazy-making space]] differs from the narcissist's shared fantasy. The narcissist's shared fantasy is about safety, stability, and re-enacting early childhood conflicts. The psychopath's space is about power: chaos makes the psychopath the only island of stability, renders her indispensable, and allows her to rule over others and make them do her bidding. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## The [[amorality-play|Amorality play]]

The female psychopath stages what the source calls an **amorality play**. In this theater, there is no good or evil, no right or wrong — only efficacy and goal attainment. People are reduced to props or two-dimensional NPCs in a video game. She converts her psychopathy into an ideology, even a religion, with everyone around her as worshippers. Without remorse, guilt, shame, or empathy, she rewards and punishes people, consumes them as raw materials, and acts mechanically, fearlessly, and impulsively, like an inexorable force of nature. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

## Comparison to male psychopaths

The source argues that female psychopaths may be more insidious than male psychopaths. Cultural stereotypes that regard women as weak, ineffectual, or maternal cause people to lower their guard, and the female psychopath leverages this blind spot. She is subtle, passive-aggressive, surreptitious, and stealthily undermines rather than overtly attacks. The male psychopath's theatrical capacity is hampered by grandiosity, and people are more defensive around men. The female psychopath, by contrast, will cling, beg, supplicate, and demean herself to capture and control others. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]

She treats victims as inert elements in her production. Victims are not seen as having grounds for complaint; suffering in her theater is framed as an honor. She acts as a cult leader and will not hesitate to annihilate people and everything dear to them. Her abuse is impersonal — people are numbers, statistics, or minerals to be extracted and molded. She may experience flashes of empathy, remorse, shame, or guilt, but these emotions appear vicarious or non-actionable, as if viewed through virtual-reality goggles rather than genuinely owned. ^[yt-uwaiowshp9i.md:9]
