# Abandonment Anxiety in Narcissism vs Borderline Personality Disorder

**Abandonment anxiety** — clinically termed *separation insecurity* — is a core feature of both pathological narcissism and borderline personality organization, but it operates through different dynamics in each. This page contrasts the two conditions, drawing on an analysis of the "twin anxieties" of the narcissist and the borderline. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## The apparent contradiction

A common question about narcissism is: if the narcissist fears abandonment, why does he devalue and discard the people closest to him? The apparent contradiction dissolves once the narcissist's relationship to the **idealized** versus the **devalued** object is understood. The narcissist dreads abandonment only by an idealized maternal object; he craves abandonment by a devalued, persecutory object. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

## Abandonment anxiety in narcissism

The narcissist's abandonment anxiety is not focused on a specific individual in the way the borderline's is. It is an ambient, general fear of being left alone, because alone the narcissist cannot maintain and propagate the Shared Fantasy — the narcissist's "living space" through which he exercises [[external-regulation|External Regulation]] (Narcissistic Supply) and partial [[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]]. A shared fantasy, by definition, must be shared. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

Abandonment is thus catastrophized not as "this person will leave me" but as a collapse of the fantasy structure itself: soon I will be left alone, unable to supply myself, unable to regulate myself, and I will fall apart. The state borders between Narcissistic Injury and Narcissistic Mortification and is a major affective feature of Narcissistic Collapse. Abandonment functions like a fever — an indicator of an underlying pathology that exposes how fragile and delusional the narcissist's world truly is. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

### Why the narcissist pushes people away

The narcissist idealizes the maternal object — intimate partners, friends, and coworkers are converted into maternal figures and treated as investment projects. During the idealization phase, the narcissist becomes emotionally attached to the idealized object and is terrified of abandonment by it; at this stage the narcissist resembles a borderline. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

This does not apply to the devalued object. When the shared fantasy moves into devaluation and discard, the object is transmogrified from an idealized, unconditionally loving figure into a **persecutory object** — the "bad mother," the sadistic, harsh inner object perceived as an immortal internal enemy bent on dissolving or destroying the narcissist. The narcissist therefore *craves* abandonment by the devalued object: abandonment becomes a purging, an act of cleansing the internal space. ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

### Twin anxieties of the narcissist

The narcissist thus possesses twin anxieties, typical of Cluster B Personality Disorders: ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

- **Abandonment anxiety** — active during the idealization phase, attached to the idealized maternal object.
- **Annihilation anxiety** — the dread of annihilation by the persecutory object during the devaluation phase, when the narcissist wants the partner gone, eradicated, erased.

## Comparison with borderline personality disorder

Both narcissists and borderlines dread abandonment and rejection, but the phenomenology differs substantially: ^[yt-82nfsr9n2xg.md:9]

| Dimension | Borderline | Narcissist |
|---|---|---|
| Separation insecurity | Proactive and dynamic; the borderline imagines and anticipates abandonment and reacts as if it had already happened | Ambient and general; a fear of being alone and unable to sustain the shared fantasy |
| Catastrophizing | Catastrophizes specific abandonment, rejection, loneliness, and humiliation | Catastrophizes loss of the shared fantasy and long-term self-regulation |
| Emotion regulation | Emotion dysregulation; overwhelmed by emotions | Lacks this borderline feature; the anxiety is tied to the fantasy structure |
| Twin anxieties | Abandonment anxiety + engulfment anxiety (approach-avoidance; runs when the partner becomes too intimate) | Abandonment anxiety (idealization) + annihilation anxiety (devaluation) |
| Causal direction | Twin anxieties *drive* the behaviors — they are the cause | The shared-fantasy algorithm dictates behaviors; the behaviors then bring on the twin anxieties |
