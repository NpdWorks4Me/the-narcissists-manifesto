## Introject Inconstancy

**Introject inconstancy** is a psychodynamic term describing the inability to maintain stable internal representations of significant other people. In the context of borderline personality organization, it is summarized as "out of sight, out of mind": the person cannot sustain introjects—internal objects, avatars, or snapshots—of meaningful others when those people are not physically present. This also includes an incapacity to trust the permanence of meaningful people in one's life. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9-9]

The concept is identified as one of five core internal processes that drive avoidance in [[Borderline Personality Disorder|borderline personality organization]]. It is often paired with the related term **object inconstancy**, and in clinical terms it provokes [[Abandonment Anxiety|abandonment anxiety]] or separation insecurity. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9-9]

Introject inconstancy is especially relevant to episodes of [[Acting Out|acting out]]. During decompensation and acting out, the partner "is not in her mind": a psychopathic self-state takes over and the person may no longer feel like herself. The phrase "out of sight, out of mind" describes this mechanism, in which the absence of a stable internal representation allows the partner to be emotionally forgotten or dropped. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9-9]

As an object-relations failure, introject inconstancy contributes to the broader relational instability seen in borderline personality organization. It is presented as one of several "inexorable, extremely painful" internal processes that, in confluence, produce the borderline's avoidant and unstable interpersonal patterns. ^[yt-q1kj8o1cc-u.md:9-9]
