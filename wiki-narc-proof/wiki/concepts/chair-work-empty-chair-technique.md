# Chair Work (Empty Chair Technique)

## Overview

Chair work, also called the empty chair technique, is a therapeutic exercise in which a person externalizes an emotion by placing it on an empty chair and engaging it in dialogue. In the source material, it is described as one of the most powerful techniques for teaching a person to externalize her emotions behaviorally and to talk about them. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The technique involves asking the person to put a specific emotion—such as anger, envy, hatred, or fear—on an empty chair and then speak to that emotion. The person may also place abandonment anxiety on the chair, interrogate it, and ask it for help. Chair work thus functions as a dialogue between the person and her own emotional state, using the empty chair as the methodology and instrument of externalization. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Use in Emotion Regulation

In the described context, chair work is part of a broader effort to help a person with borderline dynamics label her emotions, verbalize what is happening inside her, and reduce the risk of acting out, emotional dysregulation, and compensation. By externalizing emotions and dialoguing with them, the person gains a way to relate to feelings rather than being overwhelmed by them. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

The source emphasizes that this approach is taught alongside the ability to talk about emotions, not only to display them behaviorally. The empty chair is the specific instrument used to make that dialogue possible. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]

## Related Techniques

Other techniques mentioned alongside chair work in the source include [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy]] for countering negative automatic thoughts, cognitive restructuring for anger management, rigid communication protocols to prevent personal attacks, humor to diffuse anger without mocking or invalidating it, and structured routines to address mood liability. These are presented as complementary methods, with chair work identified as one of the most powerful tools for emotional externalization. ^[yt-l7xhxkpxx0e.md:9]
