# Narcissistic collapse and the overt-covert cycle

**Narcissistic collapse** is a state described in the [[Psychodynamic Diagnostic Manual]] (PDM) account of narcissistic personalities. According to the PDM, the characteristic subjective experience of narcissistic individuals is a sense of inner emptiness and meaninglessness that requires recurrent infusions of external affirmation — "narcissistic supply" — in the form of status, admiration, wealth, or success. When narcissistic individuals extract such affirmation, they may experience an internal elation known as narcissistic elation, behaving in a grandiose and arrogant manner against a sense of entitlement and treating those perceived as lower-status with contempt. When the environment fails to provide such evidence, that is known as narcissistic collapse: the individual may feel depressed, ashamed, and envious of those who succeed in attaining the status they lack. The PDM identifies this as the core clinical feature of [[covert narcissism]]. ^[yt-nmb4uke-vva.md:9]

During collapse, individuals often fantasize about unlimited success, beauty, glory, and power, and their lack of real pleasure in either work or love can be painful to witness. The PDM adds that this applies equally to successful narcissists. ^[yt-nmb4uke-vva.md:9]

## Levels of severity and malignant narcissism

The PDM places individuals with problematic narcissistic preoccupations along a continuum of severity from the neurotic to the psychotic level of organization. Toward the neurotic end, narcissistic individuals may be socially appropriate, personally successful, charming, and reasonably well adapted to family, work, and interests, although somewhat deficient in the capacity for intimacy. At more pathological levels, whether or not they are personally successful, they suffer from identity diffusion — often concealed by a grandiose self-presentation — lack an inner-directed morality, and may behave in ways that are highly destructive and toxic to other people. ^[yt-nmb4uke-vva.md:9]
