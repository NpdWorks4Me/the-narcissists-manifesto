# Knowledge Wiki

## Concepts

- **[[abandonment-and-engulfment-anxiety-as-twin-anxieties|Abandonment and Engulfment Anxiety as Twin Anxieties]]** — The paired fears of being abandoned and of being engulfed/consumed by closeness, described as the 'fuel in the borderline's engine' that drives avoidance; effective intervention must reduce one without triggering the other.
- **[[abandonment-anxiety-in-narcissism-vs-borderline-personality-disorder|Abandonment Anxiety in Narcissism vs Borderline Personality Disorder]]** — Borderlines experience proactive, catastrophic separation insecurity that drives their behavior, whereas narcissists have an ambient fear of losing the shared fantasy; in narcissism the algorithm drives behavior and the twin anxieties are consequences, not causes.
- **[[acting-out-and-subclinical-psychopathy-in-bpd|Acting Out and Subclinical Psychopathy in BPD]]** — Reckless self-protective behaviors — promiscuity, shopaholism, workaholism, substance abuse — that erupt when the borderline's defenses collapse (decompensation); a psychopathic self-state takes over, the partner drops out of mind, and the source links this to factor-two subclinical psychopathy.
- **[[affective-matching|Affective Matching]]** — Second mechanism: the individual validates their shame, guilt, and remorse by embracing them as the authentic 'cost of being me,' refusing to change behavior and instead repeating misconduct as an act of loyalty to a defiant, reckless self-image.
- **[[amorality-play|Amorality play]]** — The psychopath's personal theater in which there is no good or evil, only efficacy and goal attainment; everyone in the psychopath's life is reduced to a two-dimensional prop or NPC in a private religion-cult staged by the psychopath as auteur.
- **[[approach-avoidance-cycle-in-bpd-relationships|Approach-Avoidance Cycle in BPD Relationships]]** — The oscillating relational dynamic where the borderline approaches for intimacy, withdraws out of engulfment anxiety, then re-approaches out of abandonment anxiety — a cycle mirrored and reciprocated by the partner's own avoidant and decompensating responses.
- **[[approach-avoidance-repetition-compulsion-in-maternal-relationships|Approach-Avoidance Repetition Compulsion in Maternal Relationships]]** — A pattern in which a difficult mother who is gratified by her child's meeting of expectations feels suffocated and threatened by the implied demand to reciprocate, then flips and renders the child an enemy — so the child can never win and intimacy never reaches a stable plateau.
- **[[autoplastic-vs-alloplastic-defenses|Autoplastic vs Alloplastic Defenses]]** — Autoplastic defenses accept responsibility for one's actions, while alloplastic defenses blame others and imply an external locus of control; therapy should reward autoplastic defenses and use language shifts ('I did it' vs 'it happened to me').
- **[[borderline-abandonment-sensitivity-and-hypervigilance|Borderline Abandonment Sensitivity and Hypervigilance]]** — The borderline's constant, seismograph-like monitoring for abandonment or rejection, including catastrophizing and mislabeling normal behaviors, which leads to decompensation and secondary psychopathic acting out when abandonment is sensed.
- **[[borderline-personality-disorder-bpd-core-features|Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD) Core Features]]** — The defining constellation of BPD traits described in the source: innate emptiness, emotion dysregulation, suicidality/self-harm, chronic anger, intense unstable relationships, and twin anxieties of abandonment and engulfment — not all present in every borderline individual.
- **[[borderline-personality-disorder-as-a-subspecies-of-dissociative-identity-disorder|Borderline Personality Disorder as a Subspecies of Dissociative Identity Disorder]]** — The claim that BPD's mood lability and emotional dysregulation are outward manifestations of switching between self-states (host personality and alters), making BPD a subspecies of DID; the dissociative trigger is typically actual or threatened abandonment.
- **[[borderline-personality-disorder-as-an-invisibility-cloak|Borderline Personality Disorder as an Invisibility Cloak]]** — Metaphor that BPD can mask underlying co-morbid cluster B conditions — narcissism, histrionic personality disorder, and psychopathy — so that when borderline features soften with age, the 'true face' of the person (the other disorders) becomes glaringly apparent.
- **[[borderline-rage-vs-narcissistic-rage|Borderline Rage vs Narcissistic Rage]]** — A distinction drawn in the source: narcissistic rage is reactive to challenge or undermining, whereas borderline rage is chronic background noise — the borderline is always angry, always on the verge of fury.
- **[[cbt-for-negative-automatic-thoughts-in-bpd|CBT for Negative Automatic Thoughts in BPD]]** — Cognitive behavioral therapy counters the borderline's catastrophic negative automatic thoughts that drive anxiety and depression, which are frequent comorbidities of borderline personality disorder.
- **[[chair-work-empty-chair-technique|Chair Work (Empty Chair Technique)]]** — A therapeutic technique where the client places an emotion on an empty chair and dialogues with it to externalize and interrogate feelings like anger, envy, or abandonment anxiety.
- **[[childhood-self-blame-attribution-i-am-the-problem|Childhood self-blame attribution ('I am the problem')]]** — A child facing a dysfunctional mother cannot safely conclude the mother is the problem — since that threatens survival (feeding, shelter) — so the child instead adopts the stance that they themselves are wrong, internalizing wrongness for life and blocking later self-awareness.
- **[[cluster-b-personality-disorders|Cluster B personality disorders]]** — A family of four diagnoses in the DSM — narcissistic, borderline, antisocial (extreme form psychopathy), and histrionic personality disorder — known as the erratic or dramatic personality disorders because sufferers are unpredictable, impulsive, dramatic, and uncertain.
- **[[co-idealization-in-narcissism|Co-idealization in Narcissism]]** — When the narcissist idealizes an intimate partner or friend, he is actually idealizing himself, since the partner is a possession or status symbol (like owning a Lamborghini) that elevates the narcissist's own grandiosity.
- **[[co-morbidity-and-the-categorical-vs-dimensional-debate|Co-morbidity and the categorical vs. dimensional debate]]** — The observation that a single patient often meets criteria for multiple cluster B diagnoses (narcissist on Monday, borderline on Wednesday, psychopath on Friday), making co-occurrence a major problem for the DSM's categorical approach and driving a transition toward dimensional diagnosis.
- **[[co-morbidity-of-borderline-personality-disorder-and-psychopathy|Co-morbidity of Borderline Personality Disorder and Psychopathy]]** — BPD has a notably high co-occurrence with antisocial personality disorder (the clinical name for psychopathy), with learned estimates that roughly one in three borderlines is also a psychopath.
- **[[coercive-snapshocking|Coercive snapshocking]]** — The narcissist's externalized, reckless aggression aimed at forcing the victim to conform to and merge with the internal object representing them, escalating to verbal or physical violence.
- **[[cold-empathy-and-theory-of-mind-in-borderline-and-antisocial-disorders|Cold empathy and theory of mind in borderline and antisocial disorders]]** — The capacity shared by borderlines and antisocials to rapidly build a theory of mind about others and leverage it to manipulate — empathy deployed cognitively rather than affectively.
- **[[communication-protocols-in-relationships|Communication Protocols in Relationships]]** — Rigid rules for conflict dialogue that prohibit personal attacks and blaming, requiring partners to speak about their own internal state ('I'm in pain') rather than accusing the other person.
- **[[conscience-as-internalized-social-norms|Conscience as Internalized Social Norms]]** — Conscience is framed as an internal object containing society's prohibitions and edicts, transmitted through socialization agents such as parents; in disordered individuals these internalized brakes fail to inhibit abuse and instead become triggers.
- **[[covert-vulnerable-narcissism|Covert / vulnerable narcissism]]** — A variant of narcissism in which the person is not ego-syntonic but unhappy, resentful, envious and bitter — contrasting with overt narcissists, who are described as the most ego-syntonic of the Cluster B disorders.
- **[[covert-phase-and-the-mask-of-sanity|Covert phase and the mask of sanity]]** — A deceptive bridge between borderline and psychopathic states where the narcissist appears perfectly normal, pseudo-civil, and consensus-seeking while secretly planning detailed revenge fantasies and the victim's destruction.
- **[[crazy-making-space|Crazy-making space]]** — The psychopathic female's equivalent of the narcissist's shared fantasy — a vortex or whirlpool of chaos, drama, unpredictability and menace that sucks in concentric circles of people, empowering the psychopath as the sole island of stability.
- **[[dsm-vs-icd-diagnostic-philosophy|DSM vs ICD diagnostic philosophy]]** — The DSM is categorical and list-based, assigning distinct diagnoses and treatments per disorder, while the ICD rejects diagnosis in favor of describing and analyzing each individual and building a unique, customized treatment plan — reflecting a radical philosophical divide in how personality disorders are conceptualized.
- **[[early-trauma-and-the-development-of-borderline-personality|Early trauma and the development of borderline personality]]** — A case narrative links borderline personality development to early loss of a mother, feelings of abandonment, and multiple traumas before age 20, with no therapeutic intervention received.
- **[[ego-function-outsourcing-in-bpd|Ego Function Outsourcing in BPD]]** — The borderline outsources internal regulation to the intimate partner, who becomes an external locus of control and regulator of moods and emotions—an unhealthy dynamic that must be gradually transferred back.
- **[[emotion-dysregulation-in-bpd|Emotion Dysregulation in BPD]]** — Affective lability — the inability to control one's emotions, which overwhelm and drown the individual — cited as a core feature of borderline personality disorder.
- **[[engulfment-anxiety|Engulfment Anxiety]]** — The fear of being consumed, subsumed, or controlled by the other, experienced both by the borderline (in intimacy) and by the partner who feels smothered, suffocated, and stripped of agency.
- **[[environmental-etiology-of-cluster-b-disorders|Environmental etiology of cluster B disorders]]** — The claim, supported by 80 years of studies, that nurture — dysfunctional households, abuse, and trauma — is the major etiological contribution to cluster B disorders, with genetics/heredity contributing less than 5% of the variability and upbringing circumstances accounting for 95%.
- **[[external-emotional-regulator-role|External Emotional Regulator Role]]** — In a relationship with a borderline, the partner is reduced to an external regulator whose raison d'être is to manage the borderline's moods and emotions, rendering the partner property rather than a person with agency.
- **[[external-locus-of-control|External Locus of Control]]** — The state of being controlled and determined from outside one's self when externally regulated — an egodistonic, menacing feeling that nonetheless offers tradeoffs like absolution from responsibility.
- **[[external-regulation|External Regulation]]** — Using other people to regulate and stabilize one's own emotions and moods, typically when internal regulation fails or is resisted — common in BPD and narcissistic disorders.
- **[[external-regulation-and-authoritarianism|External Regulation and Authoritarianism]]** — The Faustian deal between authoritarian leaders and populaces, where the leader absorbs all responsibility and blame in exchange for power, freeing followers from anxiety, guilt and shame.
- **[[failure-vs-loser-distinction|Failure vs. Loser Distinction]]** — A failure never attains success, while a loser experiences success but fails to leverage it into long-term beneficial outcomes (career, marriage, raising children); the loser is not efficacious.
- **[[false-self-etiology-borderline-vs-narcissistic|False Self Etiology: Borderline vs Narcissistic]]** — The false self develops in opposite directions in the two conditions: the borderline's false self is initially external and then internalized, while the narcissist's false self is initially internal and later acquires external capacity (e.g., social interaction) — mirror images of each other.
- **[[familial-normalization-of-personality-disorder-thats-just-how-she-is|Familial normalization of personality disorder ('that's just how she is')]]** — Families often minimize a member's dysfunction with refrains like 'that's just how she is' and 'go along to get along,' discouraging help-seeking and framing rage explosions as acceptable.
- **[[female-psychopathy|Female psychopathy]]** — A distinct subtype of psychopathy concentrated in women — secondary (factor two) psychopaths marked by impulsivity, emotion dysregulation, and relational abuse, yet more insidious than male psychopaths due to cultural blind spots.
- **[[forensic-non-recognition-of-dissociative-states-as-legal-defense|Forensic Non-Recognition of Dissociative States as Legal Defense]]** — Courts worldwide reject borderline personality disorder and dissociative identity disorder as mitigating circumstances or defenses, even when a crime was committed while an alter personality was in control.
- **[[freezing-the-partner-objectification-dynamic|Freezing the Partner (Objectification Dynamic)]]** — Both narcissists and borderlines freeze their partner into an unchanging, inanimate object — the narcissist to protect the introject, the borderline to ensure object constancy and prevent the partner from evolving or leaving.
- **[[frustration-perceived-as-internal-attack|Frustration perceived as internal attack]]** — Because narcissists interact only with internal objects, frustration is misperceived as coming from inside the mind — the victim's 'avatar' attacks like a Trojan horse — so walking away cannot end the narcissist's torment.
- **[[gender-bias-and-the-female-psychopathy-hypothesis|Gender bias and the female psychopathy hypothesis]]** — The argument that BPD and histrionic PD may be culturally channeled female expressions of secondary psychopathy, and that diagnostic criteria, sampling bias, and gender-role norms distort apparent prevalence differences between men and women.
- **[[groschsteins-theory-borderlines-as-failed-narcissists|Groschstein's Theory: Borderlines as Failed Narcissists]]** — Groschstein's claim that all children first become borderlines and then try to become narcissists; those who succeed become narcissists while those who fail remain borderlines, making borderline the underlying 'false state' onto which narcissism is superimposed as a defense.
- **[[group-and-peer-pressure-as-external-regulation|Group and Peer Pressure as External Regulation]]** — Collective settings — ingroups, mob or cult minds, and peer pressure — where the group or peers control, regulate, and enforce conformity over the individual's behaviors, cognitions and emotions.
- **[[humor-as-an-antidote-to-anger|Humor as an Antidote to Anger]]** — Using non-mocking humor to expose a situation's irrationality and diffuse anger, without invalidating or minimizing the angry person's feelings.
- **[[hypervigilance-and-referential-ideation|Hypervigilance and referential ideation]]** — Hypervigilance ('thin skin') is the tendency to interpret every speech act, behavior and body language as insult, slight or attack; it is not a clinical feature of any single disorder but appears across personality disorders, referential ideation (believing others talk about you), and trauma conditions including CPTSD and PTSD where it manifests as a negative interpretive bias.
- **[[idealization-devaluation-and-approach-avoidance-in-bpd-relationships|Idealization-Devaluation and Approach-Avoidance in BPD Relationships]]** — Intense intimate relationships marked by cycles of idealization and devaluation, and approach-avoidance dynamics ('I love you, I hate you, don't leave me'), described as a key feature of borderline personality disorder.
- **[[idealization-devaluation-cycles-in-bpd|Idealization-Devaluation Cycles in BPD]]** — Rapid cycles of idealizing the partner as God and devaluing them as the devil, occurring several times a day in borderline—faster than in narcissism—during which the borderline feels no guilt about betrayal or harm.
- **[[identity-disturbance-in-borderline-personality-organization|Identity Disturbance in Borderline Personality Organization]]** — Fluctuation between mutually exclusive beliefs, values, behaviors, and emotions caused by the absence of a core identity; the borderline is described as a 'reification of absence' with an empty schizoid core, able to flip from A to minus-A from one day to the next.
- **[[impaired-self-formation-and-identity-diffusion|Impaired self-formation and identity diffusion]]** — The claim that cluster B individuals are 'selfless' — lacking a functional self/ego due to disrupted formation of the self's two components (sense of continuity and boundaries), resulting in identity diffusion or identity disturbance rather than a core identity.
- **[[innate-emptiness-in-bpd|Innate Emptiness in BPD]]** — A pervasive sense of an internal void — a black hole that is all-consuming from the inside — listed as the first significant tell of borderline personality disorder.
- **[[internal-object-interaction-introjection-as-defense|Internal Object Interaction (Introjection as Defense)]]** — Partners of individuals with borderline personality disorder develop a mental 'snapshot' or avatar of the borderline and interact with that internal object instead of the real person, as a self-preservation strategy.
- **[[internal-regulation|Internal Regulation]]** — Self-sufficient stabilization of fluctuating moods and emotions from within; healthy people are stable around the average and treat emotions as signals rather than threats.
- **[[intimacy-as-regulatory-outsourcing|Intimacy as Regulatory Outsourcing]]** — The view that intimacy is a form of external regulation: it requires exposing vulnerability and handing over some power or control to another person, with a modicum of dependency that healthy relationships keep bounded.
- **[[introject-inconstancy|Introject Inconstancy]]** — The borderline's inability to maintain stable internal representations of significant others ('out of sight, out of mind'), leading to distrust of the permanence of relationships and provoking abandonment anxiety or separation insecurity.
- **[[kernbergs-compounded-defense-theory|Kernberg's Compounded Defense Theory]]** — Kernberg's position that narcissistic personality disorder is a compounded defense against underlying borderline personality organization, i.e., narcissism is constructed to defend against borderline dynamics.
- **[[lone-wolf-vs-grandiose-self-state-in-bpd|Lone Wolf vs. Grandiose Self-State in BPD]]** — A differential diagnostic pattern: BPD patients with a psychopathic inner self-state behave as 'lone wolves' (long periods alone, avoidant), while those with a grandiose self-state cannot tolerate being alone and constantly seek an intimate partner.
- **[[mood-as-ancillary-vs-primary-clinical-feature|Mood as ancillary vs. primary clinical feature]]** — In bipolar disorder the mood disturbance is the direct driver of all symptoms, whereas in borderline personality disorder mood problems are ancillary and other clinical features — absent in bipolar — are far more relevant to the diagnosis.
- **[[mood-cycling-timescales-bipolar-vs-borderline|Mood cycling timescales: bipolar vs. borderline]]** — Bipolar disorder cycles between manic and depressive moods over weeks to months, while borderline personality disorder involves rapid mood shifts within hours, with a mood lasting four days considered an extreme outlier.
- **[[narcissism-as-a-defense-against-borderline-organization|Narcissism as a defense against borderline organization]]** — Kernberg's and Grotstein's formulations that pathological narcissism covers an underlying borderline vulnerability — narcissism as an advanced stage of or defense against borderline personality structure.
- **[[narcissism-as-the-mirror-image-of-borderline|Narcissism as the Mirror Image of Borderline]]** — The narcissistic defense is defined as the opposite of borderline: where the borderline projects internal objects outward and idealizes external objects, the narcissist neutralizes threat by appropriating, incorporating, and digesting external objects to control them internally.
- **[[narcissism-as-variant-of-borderline-personality-organization|Narcissism as Variant of Borderline Personality Organization]]** — Following Otto Kernberg, pathological narcissism is a more rigid and structured form of the borderline personality organization; where the borderline reacts to dependency with approach-avoidance repetition compulsion, the narcissist reacts psychopathically with abuse.
- **[[narcissism-worsens-with-age|Narcissism Worsens with Age]]** — Clinical observation that narcissism does not ameliorate with age; unlike some borderline features, most narcissistic traits intensify over time (with antisocial behaviors as the noted exception), and dementia can further accentuate them.
- **[[narcissist-twin-anxieties|Narcissist Twin Anxieties]]** — The narcissist experiences two opposing anxieties across the relationship cycle: abandonment anxiety during the idealization phase and annihilation anxiety during the devaluation phase.
- **[[narcissistic-abuse-as-an-idealization-test|Narcissistic Abuse as an Idealization Test]]** — Counterintuitively, narcissistic abuse serves to idealize the partner: if the partner survives abuse and remains committed, it proves their strength, resilience and capacity to love — idealized traits that render the partner worthy of the narcissist's godlike attributes.
- **[[narcissistic-collapse-and-the-overt-covert-cycle|Narcissistic collapse and the overt-covert cycle]]** — The claim that every narcissist cycles through overt and covert phases; the covert presentation seen in therapy reflects narcissistic collapse when narcissistic supply fails, not a separate subtype, and all narcissists remain grandiose in both phases.
- **[[narcissistic-decompensation|Narcissistic Decompensation]]** — The collapse of narcissistic defenses under extreme stress (e.g., narcissistic mortification or injury), causing the narcissist to default back to a borderline state with emotional dysregulation, suicidal ideation, and self-harm.
- **[[narcissistic-dissonance-and-the-resentment-ideology|Narcissistic Dissonance and the Resentment Ideology]]** — The conflict between the narcissist's godlike self-image and his humiliating need to solicit supply creates cognitive-emotional dissonance; the resulting resentment and rage crystallize into an organizing ideology that explains the world's 'cosmic injustice' toward him.
- **[[narcissistic-mortification-and-collapse|Narcissistic Mortification and Collapse]]** — Abandonment in narcissism borders between narcissistic injury and narcissistic mortification, serving as a 'fever' indicator of underlying pathology and a major affective feature of narcissistic collapse when the shared fantasy can no longer be maintained.
- **[[narcissistic-self-state-transitions|Narcissistic self-state transitions]]** — Under stress or narcissistic injury, a narcissist shifts from a narcissistic state into a borderline state, then (if frustration persists) into a primary psychopathic state, passing through a covert bridging phase.
- **[[narcissistic-supply-as-addiction|Narcissistic Supply as Addiction]]** — The narcissist's need for narcissistic supply (attention, adulation, sex, power, money) is a quasi-physiological addiction required for emotional self-regulation and survival, not mere vanity — without it the narcissist becomes disregulated and borderline-like.
- **[[narcissistic-supply-elation-and-inner-emptiness|Narcissistic supply, elation, and inner emptiness]]** — The PDM's object-relations account: narcissistic individuals experience inner emptiness and meaninglessness that requires recurrent external affirmation (narcissistic supply) — yielding narcissistic elation when obtained and depression, shame, and envy on failure.
- **[[pathological-external-regulation|Pathological External Regulation]]** — Extreme forms of regulatory outsourcing in which the intimate partner becomes an externalized ego — expected to regulate emotions and needs in BPD, the self-concept of the narcissist via narcissistic supply in codependence, and to supply shared fantasy for narcissists who cannot achieve true intimacy.
- **[[persecutory-object-persary-in-narcissism|Persecutory Object (Persary) in Narcissism]]** — The devalued partner becomes a 'persary' — an internal persecutory object representing the rejecting, sadistic 'bad mother' — an immortal internal enemy the narcissist wishes to be abandoned by and seeks to erase.
- **[[pervasive-dissociation-in-bpd|Pervasive Dissociation in BPD]]** — Bouts of amnesia, derealization, and depersonalization in which the borderline feels empty, unreal, and on autopilot — especially when alone or during acting-out sex, where she observes her body acting without feeling 'inside' it.
- **[[psychopathic-vs-narcissistic-sexuality|Psychopathic vs narcissistic sexuality]]** — The psychopath's sexuality is coercive, aggressive, and power-driven (owning and possessing the partner), whereas the narcissist's sexuality is non-coercive because the narcissist needs to feel irresistible — a contrast rooted in power being the psychopath's narcissistic supply.
- **[[reaction-formation-and-projective-identification-in-abuse|Reaction Formation and Projective Identification in Abuse]]** — Third mechanism: the abuser cannot tolerate ego-dystonic shame/guilt/remorse, so via reaction formation and projective identification they transfer these emotions onto the victim, devaluing and abusing them so the victim becomes the bearer of the disowned feelings.
- **[[reversal-of-kernbergs-hierarchy|Reversal of Kernberg's Hierarchy]]** — Vaknin's argument that the narcissist's personality is less organized and closer to psychosis than the borderline's, held together only by rigid grandiosity — reversing Otto Kernberg's proposed hierarchy.
- **[[scapegoat-and-golden-child-roles-in-the-difficult-mother-dynamic|Scapegoat and Golden Child Roles in the Difficult Mother Dynamic]]** — Adult children of difficult mothers can occupy transitory roles of scapegoat or golden child; the mother's underlying message to the scapegoat is one of constant failure — 'something's wrong with you,' 'you're a disappointment' — which can never be satisfied.
- **[[semantic-vs-episodic-psychological-maturity|Semantic vs episodic psychological maturity]]** — Semantic functions (skill acquisition, professional competence, executive function in structured environments) are independent of episodic maturity (life experience, autobiography, object relations, emotional coping). A person can be emotionally three years old — unable to perceive others as external objects — while simultaneously being a leading physicist or head of state.
- **[[shame-guilt-and-remorse-as-drivers-of-abuse|Shame, Guilt, and Remorse as Drivers of Abuse]]** — Counterintuitive thesis that in personality-disordered individuals, the inhibitory emotions of shame, guilt, and remorse can paradoxically fuel and escalate abusive behavior rather than stop it.
- **[[shared-fantasy-as-the-narcissists-operating-algorithm|Shared Fantasy as the Narcissist's Operating Algorithm]]** — The narcissist's behaviors are dictated by an inexorable, immutable algorithm called the 'shared fantasy' — a paracosm that requires at least one participant to maintain, which is why being left alone triggers collapse.
- **[[shared-features-and-differentiating-features-of-bpd-and-psychopathy|Shared features and differentiating features of BPD and psychopathy]]** — A comparison showing both disorders share impulsivity, anxious-preoccupied attachment, disinhibition, and developmental burnout by mid-life, while differing in identity coherence, emotional intensity, reality-testing, and idealization-devaluation cycles.
- **[[situational-narcissism-in-partners-of-borderlines|Situational Narcissism in Partners of Borderlines]]** — Healthy partners of borderlines become temporarily, transiently, and situationally narcissistic — interacting with an internal representation rather than the external partner — as an act of self-preservation.
- **[[splitting-and-triangulation-in-bpd-families|Splitting and Triangulation in BPD Families]]** — The pattern whereby a borderline parent casts one child as good and another as bad, constantly seeking alliances and triangulating among their own children — illustrated through the interviewee's childhood experience of a borderline mother.
- **[[stereotype-blind-spot-exploited-by-female-psychopaths|Stereotype blind spot exploited by female psychopaths]]** — Female psychopaths are more dangerous than male ones because culturally conditioned stereotypes framing women as weak, ineffectual, and effete lower people's defenses, and the female psychopath knowingly leverages this blindness to undermine stealthily rather than besiege overtly.
- **[[suicidality-and-self-harm-in-bpd|Suicidality and Self-Harm in BPD]]** — Constant suicidal ideation, an 11% completed suicide rate, and widespread self-mutilation/self-harm described as a prominent feature of borderline personality disorder.
- **[[superficial-charm-as-a-clinical-marker-of-psychopathy|Superficial Charm as a Clinical Marker of Psychopathy]]** — Superficial charm and glibness are key diagnostic features of psychopathy (only to a lesser extent of narcissism), so a mother who is charismatic and magnetic in public but monstrous behind closed doors is better characterized as psychopathic rather than borderline.
- **[[the-borderline-as-a-failed-narcissist|The Borderline as a Failed Narcissist]]** — Grostein's postulate that BPD is a failed narcissism: the narcissist successfully represses the true self leaving only the false self, whereas the borderline fails, so her true and false selves compete for control of identity and memories.
- **[[twin-anxieties-in-bpd|Twin Anxieties in BPD]]** — The mutually exclusive pair of fears in borderline personality disorder: abandonment/separation insecurity versus engulfment anxiety — fear of being consumed by intimacy, which drives the love-then-flee cycle.
- **[[two-developmental-pathways-to-cluster-b-pathology|Two developmental pathways to cluster B pathology]]** — Two trajectories leading to cluster B traits: (1) the child is cut off from reality through idolization, pedestalization, adulation, and blame-free perfection, plus instrumentalization and parentification; (2) the classical path of physical, verbal, psychological, or sexual abuse, adverse childhood experiences, neglect, and abandonment — both ending in the child's objectification.
- **[[unitary-cluster-b-hypothesis|Unitary Cluster B hypothesis]]** — The claim, attributed by the lecturer to his own work and to the ICD's approach, that all Cluster B personality disorders (narcissistic, borderline, psychopathic, histrionic) are facets of a single underlying disorder — illustrated by the parable of the blind wise men describing different parts of one elephant — and should therefore be treated identically rather than as separate DSM diagnoses.
- **[[weaponizing-abandonment-anxiety-as-relationship-leverage|Weaponizing Abandonment Anxiety as Relationship Leverage]]** — Threatening abandonment during the idealization phase can rapidly modify narcissistic behavior and enforce boundaries, but the tactic backfires catastrophically in the devaluation phase, where the narcissist craves and welcomes abandonment.
